Quantum computing implementation
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3d/Measuring_a_qubit_leaves_no_room_for_error.jpg/500px-Measuring_a_qubit_leaves_no_room_for_error.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Measuring_a_qubit_leaves_no_room_for_error.jpg)A laboratory team assembles a cryogenic part of a superconducting quantum computer. This provides necessary cooling of superconducting processors to almost absolute zero (-273.1°C).
**Superconducting quantum computing** is a branch of [quantum computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_computing "Quantum computing") and [solid-state physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid-state_physics "Solid-state physics") that implements [superconducting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductivity "Superconductivity") [electronic circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_circuit "Electronic circuit") as [qubits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qubit "Qubit") in a quantum processor. These devices are typically [microwave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave "Microwave")-frequency electronic circuits containing [Josephson junctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephson_junctions "Josephson junctions"), which are fabricated on solid state chips. 
Superconducting circuits are one of many possible physical implementations of qubits, the quantum computer's equivalent of a traditional [bit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit "Bit") in a classic [computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer "Computer").(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-1) Qubits refer to a two-state quantum mechanical system, and have two logic states, the [ground state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_state "Ground state") and the [excited state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excited_state "Excited state"), often denoted  | g ⟩ and  | e ⟩ {\displaystyle |g\rangle {\text{ and }}|e\rangle } ![{\\displaystyle |g\\rangle {\\text{ and }}|e\\rangle }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/99552065cb74418d6838761bfa2f58ed3a1fc908) (for ground and excited), or  | 0 ⟩ and  | 1 ⟩ {\displaystyle |0\rangle {\text{ and }}|1\rangle } ![{\\displaystyle |0\\rangle {\\text{ and }}|1\\rangle }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/a55df40546f65644de62173aab5bee77d975e76e).(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-docs.pennylane.ai-2) Superconducting quantum computing implementations are categorized as "solid state" quantum computers, where qubits are intrinsically integrated in a solid-state device. Solid state quantum computers also borrow fabrication techniques developed for solid state classical computation.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-3)
Superconducting architecture is the dominant method in the industry for developing quantum processing units, or QPUs. Research in superconducting quantum computing is conducted by companies such as [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google "Google"),(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-4) [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM "IBM"),(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-IBM-5) [IMEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMEC "IMEC"),(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-6) [BBN Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBN_Technologies "BBN Technologies"),(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-7) [Rigetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigetti_Computing "Rigetti Computing"),(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-8) and [Intel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel "Intel").(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-9) Alternatives to superconducting qubits include [trapped ions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trapped-ion_quantum_computer "Trapped-ion quantum computer"), and [neutral atoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_atom_quantum_computer "Neutral atom quantum computer"). 
Ongoing research in superconducting quantum computing includes device-level improvement, developing error correction methods, and demonstrating [quantum advantage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_supremacy "Quantum supremacy") by comparing a quantum processor's performance to a classical computer. 
## History
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")]
Quantum computers were first proposed by Richard Feynman, who in 1982 proposed using such a computer to simulate and understand the properties of other quantum systems. In the 1990s, two quantum algorithms were published, which further stirred interest in realizing quantum computers. [Peter Shor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Shor "Peter Shor") proposed [Shor's algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shor%27s_algorithm "Shor's algorithm"), a [quantum algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_algorithm "Quantum algorithm") for finding the [prime factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factor "Prime factor") of an integer, which could in theory break RSA encryption. Similarly, [Lov Grover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lov_Grover "Lov Grover") proposed the [Grover search algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grover%27s_algorithm "Grover's algorithm"), which provides an alternative to [binary search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_search "Binary search") that can be done with quadratic speedup. 
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ca/Keynote_-_Entering_the_Era_of_Quantum_Utility_%28IBM_Research%29_02.png/250px-Keynote_-_Entering_the_Era_of_Quantum_Utility_%28IBM_Research%29_02.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Keynote_-_Entering_the_Era_of_Quantum_Utility_\(IBM_Research\)_02.png)The [IBM Heron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Heron "IBM Heron") superconducting quantum processor pictured above in 2023, is based on [transmon qubits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmon "Transmon") and is part of one of the first [circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_circuit "Quantum circuit")-based commercial [quantum computers.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_computing "Quantum computing")
At the time, superconducting quantum circuits were already being used to construct highly sensitive [SQUID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SQUID "SQUID") devices, and had also been used to demonstrate [macroscopic quantum phenomena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroscopic_quantum_phenomena "Macroscopic quantum phenomena"), such as quantized energy levels. It became apparent that these superconducting qubits could be used to achieve quantum computation.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Qiskit-10) This was especially true because such "solid state" approaches to quantum computing were seen as far more viable than other approaches at the time, including NMR (nuclear magnetic resonance) quantum computing, due in part to the fact that existing fabrication techniques would apply.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-11)
In 1999, a paper(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-12) was published by [Yasunobu Nakamura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasunobu_Nakamura "Yasunobu Nakamura"), demonstrating the first superconducting qubit. It is a form of [Cooper pair box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_pair_box "Cooper pair box"), now known as the "charge qubit". Although the design had been proposed in 1997 by the Saclay team (including [Devoret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Devoret "Michel Devoret")), this paper was the first to show coherent control and readout, in the form of [Rabi oscillations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabi_oscillations "Rabi oscillations") between the ground and excited states of the qubit. However, even after this first result, it was unclear if superconducting qubits would be viable, and some argued that the system was not truly capable of containing quantum information.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-13) Part of the problem was that this initial design maintained coherence for less than a nanosecond, not long enough to do any calculations. 
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/21/John_M._Martinis%2C_2025_Nobel_laurate_in_physics.jpg/250px-John_M._Martinis%2C_2025_Nobel_laurate_in_physics.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:John_M._Martinis,_2025_Nobel_laurate_in_physics.jpg)[John M. Martinis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_M._Martinis "John M. Martinis"), the 2025 Nobel laureate in physics, led the team at Google Quantum AI that built the [Sycamore processor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sycamore_processor "Sycamore processor"), which, in 2019, claimed the first evidence of [quantum supremacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_supremacy "Quantum supremacy").
In the following years, several other superconducting qubits were invented, including the [phase qubit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_qubit "Phase qubit"), [flux qubit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flux_qubit "Flux qubit"), quatronium, the [transmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmon "Transmon") qubit, and the [fluxonium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluxonium "Fluxonium"). The transmon design, which has reduced sensitivity to charge noise, is now widely and primarily used in superconducting quantum computing.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Qiskit-10) Further developments in readout and design have allowed superconducting transmon qubits to reach millisecond coherence times.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-14)
Google in 2016, implemented 16 qubits to convey a demonstration of the [Fermi-Hubbard Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermi-Hubbard_model "Fermi-Hubbard model"). In another experiment, Google used 17 qubits to optimize the [Sherrington-Kirkpatrick model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherrington-Kirkpatrick_model "Sherrington-Kirkpatrick model"). In 2019, Google produced the Sycamore quantum computer which performed a task in 200 seconds that Google claimed would have taken 10,000 years on a classical computer.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-15) The task was [random circuit sampling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_circuit_sampling "Random circuit sampling"), a common benchmark for claims of "quantum supremacy" or quantum advantage. 
As of 2025, superconducting quantum processors have exceeded 1,000 qubits, the largest being [IBM Condor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Condor "IBM Condor"), a 1,121-qubit quantum processor.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-16)(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-17) In 2025, Google announced one of the first independently verifiable quantum advantages on hardware using the [Willow processor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willow_processor "Willow processor").(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-18)
## Background
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Background")]
### Quantum computing
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Quantum computing")]
Main article: [Quantum computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_computing "Quantum computing")
Classical [computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computation "Computation") models rely on physical implementations consistent with the laws of [classical mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_mechanics "Classical mechanics").(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-19) Some very small systems, or certain systems under extreme conditions, are instead described by the [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics"), which obey different sets of physical rules. 
[Quantum computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_computation "Quantum computation") is a method of constructing a quantum system for the purpose of encoding information. Applications of a quantum computer would include simulating quantum phenomena beyond the scope of classical approximation, and speeding up certain calculations, particularly those that involve an "oracle". Certain algorithms designed for quantum computers, such as [Grover Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grover_search_algorithm "Grover search algorithm") or [Shor's algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shor%27s_algorithm "Shor's algorithm"), are believed to be able to do some calculations better than their classical counterparts. 
Gate-based quantum computing is a method of quantum computing that, much like traditional computing, use [qubits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qubit "Qubit") (analogous to bits) and [quantum gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_gates "Quantum gates") (analogous to classical gates). 
#### Qubits
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Qubits")]
Main article: [Qubit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qubit "Qubit")
A qubit is any two-level system in quantum mechanics. Much like a classical [bit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit "Bit"), it is a system with two possible [states.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_state "Quantum state") However, the difference lies in the fact that because a qubit obeys the laws of quantum mechanics, it is capable of occupying a [quantum superposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_superposition "Quantum superposition") of both states. 
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4e/Simple_qubits.svg/250px-Simple_qubits.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Simple_qubits.svg)The general definition of a qubit (quantum bit) is the quantum state of a two level quantum system. 
The primary requirement for physically constructing a qubit is the ability to be able to individually address the first two states, in this case energy levels, of the system. This is difficult, as most systems contain a near-infinite number of energy levels. In superconducting quantum computers, these qubits are constructed using superconducting resonant circuits. Each superconducting qubit is essentially a nonlinear [LC circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LC_circuit "LC circuit") with a capacitor and a [Josephson junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephson_junction "Josephson junction"), a superconducting element with a nonlinear inductance.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-pennylane.ai-20) Because the circuit is non-linear, there is unequal spacing between its energy levels, allowing the first two states to be individually addressable. 
In theory, due to its nonlinearity, the qubit is affected only by photons with the energy difference required to jump from the ground state to the excited state.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-pennylane.ai-20) In practice, however, noise in the system can still cause it to leave the computational subspace. In many cases, the higher energy levels of a superconducting qubit need to be considered.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-21) This is especially true in transmons, which have weak anharmonicity by design. 
Because the circuit is superconducting, it has zero resistance, and dissipates almost no energy. However, this comes at the price of extremely low operation [temperatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperature "Temperature"). 
#### Quantum gates
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Quantum gates")]
Main article: [Quantum logic gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_logic_gate "Quantum logic gate")
A quantum gate is a generalization of a [logic gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logic_gate "Logic gate") describing the [transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformation_matrix "Transformation matrix") of qubits from their initial state to a different state, often a superposition. 
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/31/Quantum_Toffoli_Gate_Implementation.svg/330px-Quantum_Toffoli_Gate_Implementation.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Quantum_Toffoli_Gate_Implementation.svg)A three qubit [Toffoli gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toffoli_gate "Toffoli gate") (CCNOT) is implemented using a combination of single and two-qubit gates. Toffoli gates have been experimentally implemented using three superconducting [transmon qubits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmon_qubit "Transmon qubit") coupled to a microwave resonator.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-22)
In superconducting qubits, quantum gates are implemented as microscopic pulses applied to the circuit using microwave resonators. Pulses are sent through resonators capacatively coupled to the qubit, which are harmonic oscillators that are detuned from the qubit itself. By applying an external drive to the qubit, the normal unitary evolution of the system implements a single qubit gate after a certain length of time has passed.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-:2-23)
Two qubit gates, such as the _iSWAP_ gate, can be achieved through coherent exchange or parametric coupling between two qubits.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-:2-23) In coherent exchange, the transition frequency of one of the qubits is tuned such that it matches the transition frequency of the second qubit. This method relies on the frequency tunability of the qubit, and does not work in fixed-frequency cases.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-:2-23) Parametric coupling on the other hand is done by changing the coupling constant between two qubits at the sum or difference of their two frequencies.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-24)
#### Criteria for a viable quantum processor
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Criteria for a viable quantum processor")]
There are many possible physical implementations of qubits, with superconducting circuits being one of them. In order for a given implementation to be considered viable for constructing a quantum computer, one set of criterion is the [DiVincenzo's criteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DiVincenzo%27s_criteria "DiVincenzo's criteria"),(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-qc-at-davis.github.io-25) a set of criteria for the physical implementation of superconducting quantum computing. The initial five criteria ensure that the quantum computer is in line with the postulates of quantum mechanics and the remaining two pertaining to the relaying of this information over a network.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]
Superconducting qubits already meet a large number of DiVincenzo's criteria. They are already highly scalable from a [fabrication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor_device_fabrication "Semiconductor device fabrication") standpoint, they can be initialized by thermal relaxation, and single-qubit gates combined with two-qubit gates form a [universal gate set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_gates "Universal gates"). However, superconducting qubits still struggle with having short [coherence times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherence_time "Coherence time"), making preserving [quantum information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_information "Quantum information") a challenge. 
### Superconductors
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Superconductors")]
Main article: [Superconductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductivity "Superconductivity")
Superconducting qubits are circuits made from superconducting metal material. Superconductivity is a phenomenon that occurs in some metals at low temperatures where electrical current experiences zero resistance in a material, allowing the current to flow without loss of energy, and be nearly dissipation-less.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-26)
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/da/Cooper_pairs.jpg/250px-Cooper_pairs.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Cooper_pairs.jpg)In a superconductor, weakly paired electrons (red) known as Cooper pairs, can avoid disturbances when moving through the lattice.
This phenomenon occurs because the basic charge carriers are pairs of [electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron "Electron") (known as [Cooper pairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_pairs "Cooper pairs")), rather than single electrons as found in typical conductors.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-27) While single electrons are [fermions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermion "Fermion") (with half-integer spin), Cooper pairs of electrons are [bosons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boson "Boson") (with integer spin), and as such they no longer obey the [Pauli exclusion principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauli_exclusion_principle "Pauli exclusion principle"), meaning these [Cooper pairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_pair "Cooper pair") can occupy the same states. Under certain conditions, this allows them to form a state of matter known as a [Bose–Einstein condensate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bose%E2%80%93Einstein_condensate "Bose–Einstein condensate"), where all of the pairs of electrons in the condensate each occupy the same position in space and have equal [momentum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Momentum "Momentum"). In this way, there is nothing distinguishing the pairs from each other, and they occupy the same state. As a result, the electron pairs move coherently as a single wave, bypassing the disturbances in the lattice that usually cause resistance. Thus, superconductors possess near infinite [conductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conductivity_\(electrolytic\) "Conductivity \(electrolytic\)") and near zero [resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_resistance_and_conductance "Electrical resistance and conductance"). 
Superconductivity generally only occurs near [absolute zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_zero "Absolute zero"), since that is when it is more energetically favorable for electrons to pair up than repel each other. This is one of the primary reasons why superconducting qubits must be cooled to ultra cold temperatures. 
### Superconducting electrical circuits
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Superconducting electrical circuits")]
Superconducting electrical circuits are networks of [electrical elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_element "Electrical element") described by a single condensate [wave function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_function "Wave function"), wherein [charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_charge "Electric charge") flow is well-defined by some [complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_number "Complex number") [probability amplitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_amplitude "Probability amplitude"). Quantization in the circuit results from complex [amplitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amplitude "Amplitude") [continuity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function "Continuous function"), since only discrete numbers of [magnetic flux quanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_flux_quantum "Magnetic flux quantum") can penetrate a superconducting loop. Parameters of superconducting circuits are designed by setting (classical) values to the electrical elements composing them, such as [capacitance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacitance "Capacitance") or [inductance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inductance "Inductance").[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]
#### Josephson junctions
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Josephson junctions")]
Main article: [Josephson junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephson_junction "Josephson junction")
One distinguishing attribute of superconducting quantum circuits is the [Josephson junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephson_junction "Josephson junction"), an [electrical element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_element "Electrical element") which does not exist in [normal conductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductivity#Superconducting_phase_transition "Superconductivity"). The [junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_junction "Electrical junction") is a weak connection between two superconductors on either side of a thin layer of [insulator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insulator_\(electricity\) "Insulator \(electricity\)") material only a few [atoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom "Atom") thick. The resulting Josephson junction device exhibits the [Josephson effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephson_effect "Josephson effect"), whereby the condensate wave function on the two sides of the junction are weakly correlated. Current flows through the junction due to [quantum tunneling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_tunneling "Quantum tunneling"). 
The Josephson junction exhibits a nonlinear inductance, which allows for [anharmonic oscillators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anharmonicity "Anharmonicity") for which energy levels are discretized (or [quantized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_memory_compression#Compression_using_quantization "Virtual memory compression")) with nonuniform spacing between energy levels, denoted  Δ E {\displaystyle \Delta E} ![{\\displaystyle \\Delta E}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/345f0ed5ff6ec6eddc5f908d379f032c52f119c2).(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-docs.pennylane.ai-2) In contrast, the [quantum harmonic oscillator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_harmonic_oscillator "Quantum harmonic oscillator") _cannot_ be used as a qubit as there is no way to address only two of its states, given that the spacing between every energy level and the next is exactly the same. 
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d5/Single_josephson_junction.svg/250px-Single_josephson_junction.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Single_josephson_junction.svg)A single Josephson junction where C is a thin layer of insulator and A & B are (superconducting) currents with nonequivalent wave functions

#### Circuit quantization
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Circuit quantization")]
Circuit quantization is a method of obtaining a quantum mechanical description of an electrical circuit. The end result is a [Hamiltonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_\(quantum_mechanics\) "Hamiltonian \(quantum mechanics\)") describing the energy of the system, from which other properties such as the ground and excited state can be derived. 
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/64/4_Qubit_Chip%2C_10.1103.PhysRevX.5.021027_%28A._Wallraff%2C_2015%29.png/330px-4_Qubit_Chip%2C_10.1103.PhysRevX.5.021027_%28A._Wallraff%2C_2015%29.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:4_Qubit_Chip,_10.1103.PhysRevX.5.021027_\(A._Wallraff,_2015\).png)An example of a superconducting circuit with four superconducting qubits (orange), four coplanar waveguide resonators (blue), and input, output, flux, and microwave drive lines. This circuit can be described using cQED techniques.
In circuit quantization, all electrical elements in the circuit are rewritten in terms of the condensate wave function's amplitude and phase, as opposed to the [current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_current "Electric current") and [voltage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltage "Voltage"). Then, generalized [Kirchhoff's circuit laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirchhoff%27s_circuit_laws "Kirchhoff's circuit laws") are applied at every node of the circuit network to obtain the system's [equations of motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equations_of_motion "Equations of motion"). Finally, these equations of motion must be reformulated to [Lagrangian mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrangian_mechanics "Lagrangian mechanics") such that a [quantum Hamiltonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_\(quantum_mechanics\) "Hamiltonian \(quantum mechanics\)") is derived describing the total energy of the system.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]
#### Circuit quantum electrodynamics
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Circuit quantum electrodynamics")]
Main article: [Circuit quantum electrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_quantum_electrodynamics "Circuit quantum electrodynamics")
Properties of superconducting electrical circuits coupled to a resonator are described by the framework of circuit quantum electrodynamics, or cQED. Superconducting qubits generally need to be connected to a resonator in order to protect them from environmental noise, and to allow them to be coupled to each other. The cQED framework is similar to cavity QED and uses largely the same techniques. In physical implementations, the resonator is usually an on-chip coplanar waveguide readout resonator, a superconducting LC resonator, or a high purity cavity. 
## Hardware and technology
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Hardware and technology")]
Superconducting quantum computing devices are typically designed in the [radio-frequency spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio-frequency_engineering "Radio-frequency engineering"), cooled in [dilution refrigerators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilution_refrigerator "Dilution refrigerator") below 15[ mK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelvin "Kelvin") and addressed with conventional electronic instruments, e.g. [frequency synthesizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency_synthesizer "Frequency synthesizer") and [spectrum analyzers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum_analyzer "Spectrum analyzer"). Typical dimensions fall on the range of micrometers, with sub-micrometer resolution, allowing for the convenient design of a [Hamiltonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_Hamiltonian "Quantum Hamiltonian") system with well-established [integrated circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_circuit "Integrated circuit") technology. 
### Manufacturing
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Manufacturing")]
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/50/Clean_room.jpg/250px-Clean_room.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Clean_room.jpg)Fabrication of superconducting circuits takes place inside of a highly controlled cleanroom.
Manufacturing superconducting qubits follows a process involving [lithography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithography "Lithography"), depositing of metal, [etching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etching "Etching"), and controlled [oxidation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redox "Redox").(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Kjaergaard-2020-29) This process is similar, though not the same, as CMOS (Complementary Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor) fabrication used for commercial silicon computer chips.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-30) A major difference is the use of [electron-beam lithography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron-beam_lithography "Electron-beam lithography"), as opposed to optical lithographic techniques, which is hard to scale and has low yield.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-31) However, electron beams allow for a much sharper resolution, which is often necessary for certain device designs. 
The superconductor used to make superconducting circuits is usually [aluminum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminium "Aluminium"), deposited on a [silicon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon "Silicon") substrate, but can also be [niobium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niobium "Niobium") or [tantalum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tantalum "Tantalum"), both d-band superconductors.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-32)
Improvements in fabrication continue to improve the lifetime of superconducting qubits and have made significant improvements since the early 2000s.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Kjaergaard-2020-29): 4 
### Refrigeration
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Refrigeration")]
Cryogenic [dilution refrigerators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilution_refrigerator "Dilution refrigerator") are used to keep the superconducting circuits cold. They are cooled to temperatures below 15[ mK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelvin "Kelvin"). Although superconductivity itself onsets before this temperature, a large population of thermal [quasiparticles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasiparticle "Quasiparticle") exist within the circuit, which can interfere with the circuit's superconductivity. These so-called 'equilibrium quasiparticles' are exponentially suppressed at lower temperatures.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-33) Therefore, it is favorable to cool the circuit to as low of a temperature as possible. 
Inside of the dilution fridge, the superconducting circuits are connected to various filters and amplifiers that enable the qubit to be read out from observers outside of the dilution fridge. 
## Qubit types
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Qubit types")]
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: Phase, flux, and charge qubits")]
The three primary superconducting qubit archetypes are the [phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_qubit "Phase qubit"), [charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge_qubit "Charge qubit") and [flux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flux_qubit "Flux qubit") qubit. These archetypes correspond to limits of the underlying Josephson hamiltonian. Depending on what limit the hamiltonian is in, a different aspect of the qubit will be well defined. The choice of qubit archetype impacts the qubit's transition frequency, [anharmonicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anharmonicity "Anharmonicity") (or nonlinearity), and susceptibility to noise.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-34)
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/18/Energy_scales_for_qubits.png/500px-Energy_scales_for_qubits.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Energy_scales_for_qubits.png)A graph of various superconducting qubit archetypes by their Josephson energy to charging energy ratio with a legend on the right.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Hyypp%C3%A4-2022-35) The top left graphic illustrates a unimon electrical circuit.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Hyypp%C3%A4-2022-35)
Of the three archetypes, phase qubits allow the most of Cooper pairs to tunnel through the junction, followed by flux qubits, and charge qubits allow the fewest. 
#### Charge qubit
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Charge qubit")]
Main article: [Charge qubit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge_qubit "Charge qubit")
The charge qubit, also known as the [Cooper pair box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_pair_box "Cooper pair box"), possesses a Josephson to charging energy ratio on the order of magnitude  < 1 {\displaystyle <1} ![{\\displaystyle <1}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/108cec02cce68d5f12865ff93be5e54f3c2924d7). For charge qubits, different energy levels correspond to an integer number of [Cooper pairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_pair "Cooper pair") on a superconducting island (a small superconducting area with a controllable number of charge carriers).(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-36) Indeed, the first experimentally realized qubit was the Cooper pair box, achieved in 1999.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-37)
#### Flux qubit
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: Flux qubit")]
Main article: [Flux qubit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flux_qubit "Flux qubit")
The flux qubit (also known as a persistent-current qubit) possesses a Josephson to charging energy ratio on the order of magnitude  10 {\displaystyle 10} ![{\\displaystyle 10}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/4ec811eb07dcac7ea67b413c5665390a1671ecb0). For flux qubits, the energy levels correspond to different [integer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integer "Integer") numbers of magnetic flux quanta trapped in a superconducting ring. 
#### Phase qubit
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=19 "Edit section: Phase qubit")]
Main article: [Phase qubit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_qubit "Phase qubit")
The phase qubit possesses a Josephson to charge energy ratio on the order of magnitude  10 6 {\displaystyle 10^{6}} ![{\\displaystyle 10^{6}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/3ffb66117ef0a7ccf37efe85652c7b3e829f32fb). For phase qubits, energy levels correspond to different quantum charge oscillation [amplitudes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amplitude "Amplitude") across a Josephson junction, where charge and [phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ginzburg%E2%80%93Landau_theory "Ginzburg–Landau theory") are analogous to momentum and position respectively as analogous to a [quantum harmonic oscillator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_harmonic_oscillator "Quantum harmonic oscillator"). Note that in this context phase is the complex argument of the superconducting wave function (also known as the superconducting [order parameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_transition "Phase transition")), not the phase between the different states of the qubit.

In the table above, the three superconducting qubit archetypes are reviewed. In the first row, the qubit's electrical circuit diagram is presented. The second row depicts a quantum Hamiltonian derived from the circuit. Generally, the Hamiltonian is the sum of the system's [kinetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetic_energy "Kinetic energy") and [potential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_energy "Potential energy") energy components (analogous to a particle in a [potential well](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_well "Potential well")). For the Hamiltonians denoted,  ϕ {\displaystyle \phi } ![{\\displaystyle \\phi }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/72b1f30316670aee6270a28334bdf4f5072cdde4) is the superconducting wave function phase difference across the junction,  C J {\displaystyle C_{J}} ![{\\displaystyle C_{J}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/3d3f1eb1e505eb7a31d2fc30c71f9211f5b6fae4) is the capacitance associated with the Josephson junction, and  q {\displaystyle q} ![{\\displaystyle q}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/06809d64fa7c817ffc7e323f85997f783dbdf71d) is the charge on the junction capacitance. For each potential depicted, only solid wave functions are used for computation. The qubit potential is indicated by a thick red line, and schematic wave function solutions are depicted by thin lines, lifted to their appropriate energy level for clarity. 
Note that particle mass corresponds to an [inverse function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_function "Inverse function") of the circuit capacitance and that the shape of the potential is governed by regular [inductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inductor "Inductor") and Josephson junctions. Schematic wave solutions in the third row of the table show the complex [amplitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amplitude "Amplitude") of the phase variable. Specifically, if a qubit's phase is measured while the qubit occupies a particular state, there is a non-zero probability of measuring a specific value _only_ where the depicted wave function [oscillates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscillation "Oscillation"). All three rows are essentially different presentations of the same physical system. 
### Hybridizations
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=20 "Edit section: Hybridizations")]
While the three core forms of superconducting qubits (phase, charge, and flux) are historically how superconducting qubits were categorized, most modern superconducting qubits are a hybridization of these archetypes. Many hybridizations of these archetypes exist including the [fluxonium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluxonium "Fluxonium"),(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-39) [transmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmon "Transmon"),(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-40) Xmon,(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-41) and quantronium.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-42)

#### Transmon
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=21 "Edit section: Transmon")]
Main article: [Transmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmon "Transmon")
Transmons are a special type of qubit with a [shunted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shunt_\(electrical\) "Shunt \(electrical\)") capacitor specifically designed to mitigate [noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_noise "Quantum noise"). The transmon qubit model a charge-phase hybrid qubit based on the Cooper pair box.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-44) The increased ratio of Josephson to charge energy mitigates noise. The Hamiltonian for the transmon is: 
H ^ = 4 E C ( n ^ − n g ) 2 − E J cos ⁡ ϕ ^ {\displaystyle {\hat {H}}=4E_{C}\left({\hat {n}}-n_{g}\right)^{2}-E_{J}\cos {\hat {\phi }}} ![{\\displaystyle {\\hat {H}}=4E_{C}\\left\({\\hat {n}}-n_{g}\\right\)^{2}-E_{J}\\cos {\\hat {\\phi }}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/7a728cacf47c03db5a91ca44aee5f81b6c91904b)
where n is the number of Cooper pairs transferred between the island and  ϕ {\textstyle \phi } ![{\\textstyle \\phi }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/b36dc4887c86d6f01183292a5d2f984e23318c10) is the phase difference across the junction.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-45)
Two transmons can be coupled using a [coupling capacitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacitive_coupling "Capacitive coupling").(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-docs.pennylane.ai-2) For this 2-qubit system the Hamiltonian is written       H ^ = ℏ J 2 ( σ 1 x σ 2 x + σ 1 y σ 2 y ) {\displaystyle {\hat {H}}={\frac {\hbar J}{2}}(\sigma _{1}^{x}\sigma _{2}^{x}+\sigma _{1}^{y}\sigma _{2}^{y})} ![{\\displaystyle {\\hat {H}}={\\frac {\\hbar J}{2}}\(\\sigma _{1}^{x}\\sigma _{2}^{x}+\\sigma _{1}^{y}\\sigma _{2}^{y}\)}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/41461f646447bfa99bcab858ebe5591270ebca9b),
where  J {\displaystyle J} ![{\\displaystyle J}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/359e4f407b49910e02c27c2f52e87a36cd74c053) is [current density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Current_density "Current density") and  σ {\displaystyle \sigma } ![{\\displaystyle \\sigma }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/59f59b7c3e6fdb1d0365a494b81fb9a696138c36) is [surface charge density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge_density "Charge density").(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-docs.pennylane.ai-2)
Transmon qubits are the most popular design of modern superconducting qubits, and are implemented in superconducting quantum processors such as Google's [Willow processor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willow_processor "Willow processor"), a chip with 105 physical transmon qubits.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-46) Other companies that use transmon qubits include [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM "IBM"), [Rigetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigetti_Computing "Rigetti Computing"), and [IQM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IQM_Quantum_Computers "IQM Quantum Computers"). 
The physical design of a transmon qubit can vary depending on the implementation. Common transmon designs include the "transmon cross" which is shaped like an X or cross, and the pad or "paddle transmon", which contains two paddles next to each other. 
#### Transmon-like qubits
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: Transmon-like qubits")]
Many variations of the transmon design exist and are active areas of research. They aim to improve upon failings of the transmon design. 
##### Xmon
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=23 "Edit section: Xmon")]
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7e/Chip_unimon.png/500px-Chip_unimon.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Chip_unimon.png)Superconducting circuit consisting of 3 Unimons (blue color)(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-47)
The Xmon is similar in design to a transmon in that it originated based on the planar transmon model.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-48) An Xmon is essentially a tunable transmon. The major difference between transmon and Xmon qubits is the Xmon qubit is grounded with one of its capacitor pads.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-49)
##### Gatemon
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=24 "Edit section: Gatemon")]
Another variation of the transmon qubit is the Gatemon. Like the Xmon, the Gatemon is a tunable variation of the transmon. The Gatemon is tunable via [gate voltage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threshold_voltage "Threshold voltage"). 
##### Unimon
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=25 "Edit section: Unimon")]
The Unimon consists of a single Josephson junction shunted by a linear inductor (possessing an inductance not depending on current) inside a (superconducting) [resonator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resonator "Resonator").(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Buchanan-2022-50) Unimons have increased anharmonicity and display faster operation time resulting in lower susceptibility to noise errors.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Buchanan-2022-50) Unimon qubits also have decreased susceptibility to flux noise and complete insensitivity to dc charge noise.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Hyypp%C3%A4-2022-35) However, unimon qubits have a limited tunability range. 
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0b/Fluxonium_Qubit.png/250px-Fluxonium_Qubit.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Fluxonium_Qubit.png)The left-most image shows a fluxonium superconducting loop consisting of a collection of larger area Josephson junctions and one smaller area Josephson junction, as shown by an electron microscope.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Cottet_6383-51) The top right image depicts fluxonium circuit components, and the bottom right image depicts a smaller area Josephson junction.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Cottet_6383-51)
The unimon qubit was first formulated in 2022 by researchers from [IQM Quantum Computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IQM_Quantum_Computers "IQM Quantum Computers"), [Aalto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalto_University "Aalto University"), and [VTT Technical Research Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VTT_Technical_Research_Centre_of_Finland "VTT Technical Research Centre of Finland") of Finland, and is still largely in the research stages of design.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-52)
#### Fluxonium
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=26 "Edit section: Fluxonium")]
Main article: [Fluxonium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluxonium "Fluxonium")
Fluxonium qubits are a specific type of flux qubit whose Josephson junction is shunted by a linear inductor of  E J ≫ E L {\displaystyle E_{J}\gg E_{L}} ![{\\displaystyle E_{J}\\gg E_{L}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/4e465525eaa9113f9e13ac432b79a25a725ff94a) where  E L = ( ℏ / 2 e ) 2 / L {\displaystyle E_{L}=(\hbar /2e)^{2}/L} ![{\\displaystyle E_{L}=\(\\hbar /2e\)^{2}/L}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/28f9c0b95c3136723fa7a9b7d1c85c340039645d).(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Nguyen-2019-53) In practice, the linear inductor is usually implemented by a Josephson junction array that is composed of a large number (can be often  N > 100 {\displaystyle N>100} ![{\\displaystyle N>100}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/1e3255bc173032b9c32804d14eaf7e4ccdd83cf2)) of large-sized Josephson junctions connected in a series. Under this condition, the Hamiltonian of a fluxonium can be written as:       H ^ = 4 E C n ^ 2 + 1 2 E L ( ϕ ^ − ϕ e x t ) 2 − E J cos ⁡ ϕ ^ {\displaystyle {\hat {H}}=4E_{C}{\hat {n}}^{2}+{\frac {1}{2}}E_{L}({\hat {\phi }}-\phi _{\mathrm {ext} })^{2}-E_{J}\cos {\hat {\phi }}} ![{\\displaystyle {\\hat {H}}=4E_{C}{\\hat {n}}^{2}+{\\frac {1}{2}}E_{L}\({\\hat {\\phi }}-\\phi _{\\mathrm {ext} }\)^{2}-E_{J}\\cos {\\hat {\\phi }}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/5e471e9ddb9fde229ae8038d77a6e0985c493096).
One important property of the fluxonium qubit is the longer [qubit lifetime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherence_\(physics\)#Quantum_coherence "Coherence \(physics\)") at the half flux sweet spot, which can exceed 1 millisecond.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Nguyen-2019-53)(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-54) Another crucial advantage of the fluxonium qubit when biased at the sweet spot is its large [anharmonicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anharmonicity "Anharmonicity"). In this context, anharmonicity refers to the unequal spacing of energy levels in a superconducting circuit. Large anharmonicity is beneficial because it allows fast local microwave control and mitigates spectral crowding problems, leading to better scalability.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Nguyen-2020-55)(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Nguyen-2022-56)
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=27 "Edit section: 0-π qubit")]
The 0-π qubit is a protected qubit design where logical states are protected by circuit symmetry.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-57) The logical states of the qubit are exponentially protected against relaxation and exponentially (first-order) protected to first order against dephasing due to charge (flux) noise. This ideal behavior, however, is not always realistic because it requires that parameter dispersion among nominally identical circuit elements vanishes.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-58)
## Operation and readout
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=28 "Edit section: Operation and readout")]
### Single qubits
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=29 "Edit section: Single qubits")]
The [GHz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertz "Hertz") energy gap between energy levels of a superconducting qubit is designed to be compatible with available electronic equipment, due to the [terahertz gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terahertz_gap "Terahertz gap") (lack of equipment in the higher [frequency band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency_band "Frequency band")). The [superconductor energy gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BCS_theory#Implications "BCS theory") implies a top limit of operation below ~1THz beyond which Cooper pairs break, so energy level separation cannot be too high. On the other hand, energy level separation cannot be too small due to cooling considerations: a temperature of 1 K implies [energy fluctuations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_fluctuations "Thermal fluctuations") of 20 GHz. Temperatures of tens of millikelvins are achieved in [dilution refrigerators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilution_refrigerator "Dilution refrigerator") and allow qubit operation at a ~5 GHz energy level separation. Qubit energy level separation is frequently adjusted by controlling a dedicated [bias current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biasing "Biasing") line, providing a "knob" to fine tune the qubit parameters.

### Multiple qubits
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=31 "Edit section: Multiple qubits")]
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/31/Quantum_Toffoli_Gate_Implementation.svg/500px-Quantum_Toffoli_Gate_Implementation.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Quantum_Toffoli_Gate_Implementation.svg)A three-qubit Toffoli gate is composed from one- and two-qubit gates. It requires a two-qubit gate called the "CNOT" gate.
The ability to couple qubits is essential for implementing 2-qubit [gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_logic_gate "Quantum logic gate"). Coupling two qubits can be achieved by connecting both to an intermediate electrical coupling circuit. The circuit may be either a fixed element (such as a capacitor) or be controllable (like the [DC-SQUID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SQUID#DC_SQUID "SQUID")). In the first case, [decoupling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decoupling_\(electronics\) "Decoupling \(electronics\)") qubits during the time the gate is switched off is achieved by tuning qubits out of resonance one from another, making the energy gaps between their computational states different.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-61) This approach is inherently limited to nearest-neighbor coupling since a physical electrical circuit must be laid out between connected qubits. Notably, [D-Wave Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-Wave_Systems "D-Wave Systems")' nearest-neighbor coupling achieves a highly connected [unit cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_cell "Unit cell") of 8 qubits in Chimera graph configuration. [Quantum algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_algorithm "Quantum algorithm") typically require coupling between arbitrary qubits. Consequently, multiple [swap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_gate#Swap_gate "Quantum gate") operations are necessary, limiting the length of quantum computation possible before processor [decoherence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_decoherence "Quantum decoherence"). 
#### Heisenberg interactions
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=32 "Edit section: Heisenberg interactions")]
The [Heisenberg model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_Heisenberg_model "Quantum Heisenberg model") of interactions, written as

serves as the basis for analog quantum simulation of spin systems and the primitive for an expressive set of quantum gates, sometimes referred to as _fermionic simulation_ (or _fSim_) gates. In superconducting circuits, this interaction model has been implemented using flux-tunable qubits with flux-tunable coupling,(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Foxen_Neill_Dunsworth_Roushan_2020_p.-62) allowing the demonstration of quantum supremacy.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Arute_Arya_Babbush_Bacon_2019_pp._505%E2%80%93510-63) In addition, it can also be realized in fixed-frequency qubits with fixed-coupling using microwave drives.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Nguyen-2024-64) The fSim gate family encompasses arbitrary XY and ZZ two-qubit unitaries, including the iSWAP, the CZ, and the SWAP gates (see [Quantum logic gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_logic_gate "Quantum logic gate")). 
#### Quantum bus
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=33 "Edit section: Quantum bus")]
Another method of coupling two or more qubits is by way of a [quantum bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_bus "Quantum bus"), by pairing qubits to this intermediate. A quantum bus is often implemented as a [microwave cavity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave_cavity "Microwave cavity") modeled by a quantum harmonic oscillator. Coupled qubits may be brought in and out of resonance with the bus and with each other, eliminating the nearest-neighbor limitation. Formalism describing coupling is [cavity quantum electrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavity_quantum_electrodynamics "Cavity quantum electrodynamics"). In cavity quantum electrodynamics, qubits are analogous to atoms interacting with an [optical photon cavity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_cavity "Optical cavity") with a difference of GHz (rather than the THz regime of electromagnetic radiation). Resonant excitation exchange among these artificial atoms is potentially useful for direct implementation of multi-qubit gates.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Khazali-2020-65) Following the dark state [manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifold "Manifold"), the Khazali-Mølmer scheme(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Khazali-2020-65) performs complex multi-qubit operations in a single step, providing a substantial shortcut to the conventional circuit model.

#### Geometric phase gate
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=35 "Edit section: Geometric phase gate")]
Higher levels (outside of the computational subspace) of a pair of coupled superconducting circuits can be used to induce a geometric phase on one of the computational states of the qubits. This leads to an entangling conditional phase shift of the relevant qubit states. This effect has been implemented by flux-tuning the qubit spectra (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-DiCarlo_Chow_Gambetta_Bishop_2009_pp._240%E2%80%93244-67) and by using selective microwave driving.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Ficheux_Nguyen_Somoroff_Xiong_2021_p.-68) Off-resonant driving can be used to induce differential ac-Stark shift, allowing the implementation of all-microwave controlled-phase gates.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Xiong_Ficheux_Somoroff_Nguyen_2022_p.-69)
### Qubit readout
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=36 "Edit section: Qubit readout")]
Architecture-specific readout, or [measurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_measurement "Quantum measurement"), mechanisms exist. Readout of a phase qubit is explained in the [qubit archetypes table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#Qubit_archetypes) above. A flux qubit state is often read using an adjustable DC-[SQUID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SQUID "SQUID") [magnetometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetometer "Magnetometer"). States may also be measured using an [electrometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrometer "Electrometer").(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-docs.pennylane.ai-2) A more general readout scheme includes a coupling to a microwave [resonator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resonator "Resonator"), where resonance frequency of the resonator is dispersively shifted by the qubit state.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-NatRev2017-70)(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Dispersive_Readout-71) Multi-level systems (qudits) can be readout using electron shelving.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Cottet_Xiong_Nguyen_Lin_2021_p.-72)
## Performance
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=37 "Edit section: Performance")]
### Criteria for quantum computation
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=38 "Edit section: Criteria for quantum computation")]
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=39 "Edit section: DiVincenzo's criteria")]
Main article: [DiVincenzo's criteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DiVincenzo%27s_criteria "DiVincenzo's criteria")
[DiVincenzo's criteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DiVincenzo%27s_criteria "DiVincenzo's criteria") is a list describing the requirements for a physical system to be capable of implementing a logical qubit. DiVincenzo's criteria is satisfied by superconducting quantum computing implementation. Much of the current development effort in superconducting quantum computing aims to achieve interconnect, control, and [readout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Readout_integrated_circuit "Readout integrated circuit") in the 3rd dimension with additional [lithography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithography "Lithography") layers. The list of DiVincenzo's criteria for a physical system to implement a logical qubit is satisfied by the implementation of superconducting qubits. Although DiVincenzo's criteria as originally proposed consists of five criteria required for physically implementing a quantum computer, the more complete list consists of seven criteria as it takes into account communication over a computer network capable of transmitting quantum information between computers, known as the "quantum internet". Therefore, the first five criteria ensure successful quantum computing, while the final two criteria allow for quantum communication. 
  1. **A scalable physical system with well characterized qubits.** "Well characterized" implies that the [Hamiltonian function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_mechanics "Hamiltonian mechanics") must be well-defined (i.e. the energy eigenstates of the qubit should be able to be quantified). A "scalable system" indicates that this ability to regulate a qubit should be augmentable for multiple more qubits. However, as more qubits are implemented, it leads to an exponential increase in cost and other physical implementations which pale in comparison to the enhanced speed it may offer.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-qc-at-davis.github.io-25) As superconducting qubits are fabricated on a chip, the many-qubit system is readily scalable. Qubits are allocated on the 2D surface of the chip. The demand for well characterized qubits is fulfilled with (a) qubit non-linearity (accessing only two of the available energy levels) and (b) accessing a single qubit at a time (rather than the entire many-qubit system) by way of per-qubit dedicated control lines or frequency separation, or tuning out, of different qubits.
  2. **Ability to initialize the state of qubits to a simple fiducial state.**(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-DiVincenzo-2008-73) A fiducial state is one that is easily and consistently replicable and is useful in quantum computing as it may be used to guarantee the initial state of qubits. One simple way to initialize a superconducting qubit is to wait long enough for the qubits to relax to the ground state. Controlling qubit potential with tuning knobs allows faster initialization mechanisms.
  3. **Long relevant decoherence times.**(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-DiVincenzo-2008-73) Decoherence of superconducting qubits is affected by multiple factors. Most decoherence is attributed to the quality of the Josephson junction and imperfections in the chip substrate. Due to their mesoscopic scale, superconducting qubits are relatively short lived. Nevertheless, thousands of gate operations have been demonstrated in these many-qubit systems.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-74) Recent strategies to improve device coherence include purifying the circuit materials and designing qubits with decreased sensitivity to noise sources.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Nguyen-2019-53)
  4. **A "universal" set of quantum gates.**(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-DiVincenzo-2008-73) Superconducting qubits allow arbitrary rotations in the Bloch sphere with pulsed microwave signals, implementing single qubit gates.  σ z σ z {\displaystyle \sigma _{z}\sigma _{z}} ![{\\displaystyle \\sigma _{z}\\sigma _{z}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/626e9b8e9b0044484f46fa921beb62992bedcc15) and  σ x σ x {\displaystyle \sigma _{x}\sigma _{x}} ![{\\displaystyle \\sigma _{x}\\sigma _{x}}](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/9d0d710a5fc24d23b701efce7fd4487182a50a79) couplings are shown for most implementations and for complementing the universal gate set.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-75)(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-76)(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Nguyen-2024-64) This criterion may also be satisfied by coupling two transmons with a coupling capacitor.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-docs.pennylane.ai-2)
  5. **Qubit-specific measurement ability.**(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-DiVincenzo-2008-73) In general, single superconducting qubits are used for control or for measurement.
  6. **Interconvertibility of stationary and flying qubits.**(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-DiVincenzo-2008-73) While stationary qubits are used to store information or perform calculations, flying qubits transmit information macroscopically. Qubits should be capable of converting from being a stationary qubit to being a flying qubit and vice versa.
  7. **Reliable transmission of flying qubits between specified locations.**(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-DiVincenzo-2008-73)


The final two criteria have been experimentally proven by research performed by [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich "ETH Zurich") university with two superconducting qubits connected by a [coaxial cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coaxial_cable "Coaxial cable").(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-77)
## Challenges
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=40 "Edit section: Challenges")]
Many current challenges faced by superconducting quantum computing lie in the field of microwave engineering.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-NatRev2017-70) Some challenges in superconducting qubit design are shaping the potential well and choosing particle mass such that energy separation between two specific energy levels is unique, differing from all other interlevel energy separation in the system, since these two levels are used as logical states of the qubit. Other challenges are mitigating sources of noise in the system. Finally, even more challenges occur a result of scaling to larger and larger device sizes. 
### Noise
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=41 "Edit section: Noise")]
Superconducting quantum computing must mitigate [quantum noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_noise "Quantum noise") (disruptions of the system caused by its interaction with an environment) as well as [leakage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leakage_\(machine_learning\) "Leakage \(machine learning\)") (information being lost to the surrounding environment). One way to reduce leakage is with [parity measurements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parity_measurement "Parity measurement").(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Kjaergaard-2020-29) Another strategy is to use qubits with large anharmonicity.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Nguyen-2020-55)(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Nguyen-2022-56)
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=42 "Edit section: Two-level system \(TLS\) effects")]
Two-level system (TLS) effects are a dominant source of noise in superconducting qubits.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-:0-78)(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-79) TLS act as resonant, two-level absorbers which drain energy from the qubit, significantly reducing [coherence times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherence_time "Coherence time"). They are thought to be caused by deformities during the fabrication process, and surface amorphous oxides that form on or near [Josephson junctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephson_junction "Josephson junction"). Additionally, coherent TLS defects fluctuate in time, and at the moment, mitigating them requires full recalibration of quantum processors containing 100 qubits around once per day.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-:0-78) As the TLS density increases, it becomes harder to protect a system from TLS effects.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-:0-78)
Attempts to mitigate TLS effects include developing new [fabrication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor_device_fabrication "Semiconductor device fabrication") techniques, and experimenting with new materials such as [niobium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niobium "Niobium") and [tantalum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tantalum "Tantalum").(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-80)
#### Quasiparticles
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=43 "Edit section: Quasiparticles")]
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/Electron-hole-pair.jpg/250px-Electron-hole-pair.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Electron-hole-pair.jpg)A semiconductor can be excited to create an electron hole pair. This kind of quasiparticle is a source of noise in superconducting qubits.
Quasiparticles are single-electron excitations that occur when [Cooper pairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_pair "Cooper pair") break. They consist of a superposition of an electron and an '[electron hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_hole "Electron hole")'. They occur when a Cooper pair is hit by a photon, causing it to break.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-:1-81) There are two kinds of particles; thermally-generated equilibrium quasiparticles, and non-equilibrium quasiparticles which get excited due to other effects in the system. While equilibrium quasiparticles can be suppressed exponentially by operating at low temperatures, but non-equilibrium quasiparticles, due to [radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiation "Radiation") such as gammas and cosmic ray muons, cannot.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-:1-81)(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-82)
Attempts to mitigate quasiparticle generation include increasing shielding against radiation,(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-83) quasiparticle trapping, gap engineering,(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-84) or otherwise removing them from the system.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-85)
### Scaling
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Superconducting_quantum_computing&action=edit&section=44 "Edit section: Scaling")]
As superconducting quantum computing approaches larger scale devices, researchers face difficulties in [qubit coherence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_coherence "Quantum coherence"), scalable [calibration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calibration "Calibration") software, efficient determination of [fidelity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidelity_of_quantum_states "Fidelity of quantum states") of quantum states across an entire chip, and qubit and gate fidelity.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Kjaergaard-2020-29) Moreover, superconducting quantum computing devices must be reliably reproducible at increasingly large scales such that they are compatible with these improvements.(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconducting_quantum_computing#cite_note-Kjaergaard-2020-29)
